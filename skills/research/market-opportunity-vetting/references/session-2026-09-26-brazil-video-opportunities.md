# Session evidence: Brazil video-service sweep (2026-09-26)

## Access path
The configured web search and web extraction backends were unavailable. One diagnostic web-search attempt returned `No web search provider configured`; research continued with browser navigation, Bing only for discovery, and direct official pages. Bing's broad queries were noisy and often returned irrelevant country pages, so snippets were not used as final proof.

## Official proof captured

- Homepage: https://www.opus.pro/
  - Rendered claim: “Used by 20M+ creators and businesses.”
  - Product: turns long videos into shorts, publishes to social platforms, supports API/workflow automation, captions, reframing, and dubbing.
- Customer stories: https://www.opus.pro/customer-stories
  - Rendered claim: “Used by 16M+ creators and businesses.”
  - Named organizations/creators include Audacy, HubSpot, GitHub, Univision, Commanders, Chili Piper, and Think Media.
  - Audacy case-study title claims “1B+ Views.”
- Pricing: https://www.opus.pro/pricing
  - Starter: $15/month; Pro: $29/month shown in monthly billing; Business has custom pricing.
  - Pro includes video dubbing and limited API access; Business includes API/custom integrations.
- Calibration: retain the exact page-specific 20M and 16M claims; do not reconcile them into one number.

  - Supports 175+ languages and dialects, voice-synced avatars, localization, and video generation.

- Calibration: official self-reported adoption plus visible paid plans; revenue remains unverified.

## Brazil-fit conclusion
A narrow PT-BR managed layer remains more defensible than a broad AI-video platform: upload/URL -> localized clips or dubbed video -> WhatsApp/download delivery. Wedges are PT-BR tone/terminology QA, WhatsApp-heavy handoff, Pix/card/boleto billing, vertical packaging for creators/courses/SMBs, and LGPD-aware retention/deletion. The local-incumbent search was noisy; use only the calibrated conclusion “no obvious dominant local leader found in this sweep,” not “no competitors exist.”

## Exclusions from this run
Avoid generic AI receptionists, meeting-note tools, ad-creative generators, support bots, and product-photo optimization unless a later run finds a materially sharper Brazil wedge; these categories were treated as crowded or integration-heavy.
